Specialist waitlist — patient & staff journeys

Same five moments, two actors. Toggle to compare today's phone-based process against the web prototype.

Manual process Web prototype
Patient
1

Needs an appointment

Calls the office and learns there are no open slots — told to expect a two-plus week wait.

Requests to join the specialist's waitlist on the site. No call needed.

2

On the waitlist

Has no way to check where they stand. Must call and wait on hold to ask.

Doesn't see a live position in this iteration — that's planned next — but their entry is one accurate shared record, not a note staff might lose.

3

Waiting

Waits with no updates, sometimes calling back just to confirm they're still on the list.

Still waits without a self-check — but staff can find their entry instantly instead of digging through notes.

4

Slot opens

Staff try to reach them by phone. If they miss the call, the slot moves on without them knowing.

If digital: gets a bell notification and banner. If telephone preference: no app notification — staff call them directly.

5

Books the appointment

If reached in time, confirms verbally over the phone.

If digital: taps Accept, confirms, done — no call. If telephone: tells staff on the call, and staff record it for them.

Staff
1

New request

Answers the call and writes the patient's info into a notebook or shared sheet.

Patient joins digitally, or staff add them on request — either way, a new entry appears on the dashboard instantly.

2

Tracking the list

Maintains the waitlist by memory, notes, or a sheet that's easy to lose track of.

Sees the full waitlist, in order, with status and contact preference for every patient in one view.

3

Slot opens

Notices a cancellation and has to work out who's next from scattered notes.

The dashboard shows exactly who's next in line. Staff release the slot to them with one click — nothing goes out automatically.

4

Notifying the patient

Calls the next patient on the list, then the one after that if there's no answer.

If digital: the patient's notified in-app automatically. If telephone preference: the row is flagged "requires a call," and staff call them directly.

5

Closing it out

Books the appointment over the phone and manually updates the paper or shared list.

Accepted (by the patient, or recorded by staff after a call) means booked. Declined or unanswered means staff click to pass it to the next patient — never automatic.

Edge case not shown above: if the waitlist is empty when a patient joins, steps 2–3 happen instantly — they're notified of the open slot right away rather than waiting.
